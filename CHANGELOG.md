# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Interest projection in scenarios (#226). `InterestProjection` joins the `Modification` union of `src/domain/modification.ts`, with its schema `interestProjectionParameters` in `src/domain/modification-parameters.ts`: a savings account, an annual rate of 0 to 10000 basis points, a frequency (`monthly` or `yearly`) and a start date. An account of another kind is the problem `accountKind`, and a start date on or before today `dateNotAfterToday` (owner, 27 Nov 2026). `balanceAwareSteps` now gives `creditsOn`: from the start date, it sums the end-of-day balances of the account after the transfers of the day, a negative day counting as zero, and on the last day of each month (`monthly`) or on 31 December (`yearly`) credits `round(sum x rate / (10000 x 365))`, computed with `BigInt` and `divideRoundBig`, then starts a new period; a period cut by the end of the range is not credited. The credit is never limited by a ceiling and earns interest from the next period. It is not part of the `flows` of the result, so the total cost leaves it out (D10). Nothing in the ledger, the pages or the charts uses it yet (ADR-011). See DOMAIN, Inputs of each modification type (D7, type 6) and Interest (D9).

- Savings goals with a monthly amount in scenarios (#225). `SavingsGoal` joins the `Modification` union of `src/domain/modification.ts`, with its schema `savingsGoalParameters` in `src/domain/modification-parameters.ts`: a source account, a savings account (different from the source), the date of the first transfer, then either a monthly amount with an optional end date on or after it, or a target amount and a target date on or after it. The target mode is accepted but applies nothing yet (#227). `ModificationContext` gains `accountKinds` (from the optional `accountKinds` of `SimulationInput`): a savings account of another kind, or of no known kind, is the new problem reason `accountKind`, and a first transfer on or before today is `dateNotAfterToday`. `balanceAwareSteps` gives stage B its transfers: one per month on the day of the first transfer (clamped to the last day of shorter months), up to the end date or the end of the range, goals ordered by position. The projection loop routes each one along the overflow chain of the savings account with the router of planned transfers; what fits nowhere stays in the source account, which may go below zero. `evaluateScenario` returns `goals`: for each valid goal, its monthly amount, the routing of its transfers, the total left unplaced (all of a transfer on an invalid chain, a cycle for instance) and the status `applied` (owner, 27 Nov 2026). An invalid goal is reported in `problems` only. `applyFlowModifications` also returns `applied`, the valid modifications in the order they applied.

- Income change in scenarios (#224). `IncomeChange` joins the `Modification` union of `src/domain/modification.ts`, with its schema `incomeChangeParameters` in `src/domain/modification-parameters.ts`: a target (an existing planned item, or a new income with an account and an optional category), a change (a positive integer amount, or an end, refused for a new income) and an effective date. For an existing target, every occurrence whose nominal date is on or after the effective date takes the new fixed amount, replacing a rolling-average estimate too, or is removed for an end; a planned occurrence takes the new amount as its remaining, a partial one keeps what its linked sum leaves of the new amount (`provisionRemaining`, now exported from `src/domain/occurrence-resolution.ts`), and a settled one, or a provision whose period has passed, keeps 0. A new income adds, through `nominalDates`, an external inflow of the amount every month on the day of the effective date up to the range end, with the source `modification`. Flows added by other modifications are left alone. A target that is not an income is the problem `plannedItemKind`, and an effective date not after today `dateNotAfterToday`. Nothing in the ledger, the pages or the charts uses it yet (ADR-011). See DOMAIN, Inputs of each modification type (D7, type 4).

- Loans and instalments in scenarios (#223). `loanSchedule` (`src/domain/loan.ts`) gives the payments of a principal over 1 to 600 months: their dates repeat `monthly_on_day` on the day of the date received, from the month after it, clamped to the last day of shorter months; with a zero rate, each payment is `floor(principal / months)` and the last one takes the remainder; otherwise the regular payment comes from the annuity formula, the only floating-point step of the engine, rounded once, each interest is `divideRound(remaining x annual rate in basis points, 120000)` in integers (with `BigInt` past the safe range, through `divideRoundBig`, now shared from `src/domain/money.ts`), and the last payment is what remains plus its interest, so the principal parts add up to the principal exactly; the regular payment is never below the first interest, no payment is more than what remains plus its interest, and `divideRoundBig` now follows the signs and refuses a zero denominator like `divideRound`. `Loan` joins the `Modification` union, with its schema `loanParameters` (positive integer principal kept within the safe integer range with a month of interest, 1 to 600 months, 0 to 10000 basis points, a date received, a paying account, an optional receiving account and an optional category). It adds the principal as an external inflow without a category on the date received when a receiving account is set, then each payment as an external outflow of the paying account with the category of the loan, keeping only the flows dated inside the range and skipping payments of zero. A date received not after today is the problem `dateNotAfterToday`. Nothing in the ledger, the pages or the charts uses it yet (ADR-011). See DOMAIN, Inputs of each modification type (type 3) and Rounding of the engine (D4 rule 3).

- Subscription change in scenarios (#222). `SubscriptionChange` joins the `Modification` union of `src/domain/modification.ts`, with its schema `subscriptionChangeParameters` in `src/domain/modification-parameters.ts`: a target (an existing planned item, or a new subscription with an account and an optional category), a mode (`monthly`, `yearly` or `none`), a positive integer amount required with a rhythm and refused with `none`, and a start date. From the start date, every occurrence of an existing target whose nominal date is on or after it is removed, whatever its displayed date after an override or a business-day shift; then, unless the mode is `none`, the new dates come from `nominalDates` (`monthly_on_day` on the day of the start date, clamped to the last day of shorter months, or `yearly` anchored on it) up to the range end, each an external outflow of the amount with the category of the target and the source `modification`. A change never removes the flows another modification added, including an earlier change on the same target. A start date not after today is the problem `dateNotAfterToday`, a target that is not an expense `plannedItemKind`, a missing one `plannedItemMissing`, and the account of an existing target is checked like any other reference (`accountMissing`, `accountArchived`): `references` of each transform now receives the context. Nothing in the ledger, the pages or the charts uses it yet (ADR-011). See DOMAIN, Inputs of each modification type (D7, type 1).

- Scenario modifications, one-off purchase (#221). `src/domain/modification.ts` defines the modification model (`MODIFICATION_TYPES`, the `Modification` union with its first member `OneOffPurchase`, `ModificationProblem` and its reasons) and stage A of the engine: `applyFlowModifications` walks the modifications by position, then id, checks the parameters of each against the schema of its type, then its references (a missing or archived account, a missing category, a missing planned item), records the reason and skips it when invalid, and otherwise calls the transformation of its type from a dispatch table; an invalid modification never throws. `src/domain/modification-parameters.ts` holds one Zod schema per type (`oneOffPurchaseParameters`: positive integer amount in minor units, calendar date, integer ids, no unknown key), `parseModificationParameters`, which refuses a type without a schema yet as `parametersInvalid`, and `modificationLabelSchema` (1 to 80 characters once trimmed). A one-off purchase adds one external outflow of its amount on its date, with its category and the source `modification`, when the date is inside the range; a date not after today is the problem `dateNotAfterToday`, checked before the range. `evaluateScenario` (`src/domain/simulation.ts`) applies stage A to the planned occurrences and the estimates, then makes one call to `projectWithRouting` with the resulting flows as simulated flows, never mixed with the real ones, and returns the problems with the result; `SimulationInput` gains an optional `categoryIds`, and without it every categorized modification is invalid. `evaluateConstantPace` is the scenario with no modification, its outputs unchanged. Nothing in the ledger, the pages or the charts uses it yet (ADR-011). See DOMAIN, Scenario evaluation pipeline (D5, D6, D7).

- Simulation indicators (#220). `computeIndicators` (`src/domain/simulation-indicators.ts`) reads a `SimulationResult` and returns, in the reference currency: the final balance, the minimum balance from today to the end of the horizon with the first day it is reached, the savings at a chosen date, the month-end checking and savings totals of each month of the horizon (a partial last month ends on the range end), and the external outflows (every flow to outside the user's accounts dated from today to the end, as a positive sum). Every total adds the active accounts of every kind, converted with `convertAmount` at the rate of today; accounts of kind `other` count only in the totals, archived accounts are left out, and an account whose currency has no rate is left out of every total, with its currency named once in `missingRates`. A date that is not a calendar date, a today or a savings date outside the range, or a simulation that misses a day of the range, is refused with a `RangeError`. `totalCost` gives the scenario's external outflows minus the reference's, positive when the scenario costs more. Nothing in the ledger, the pages or the charts uses it yet (ADR-011). See DOMAIN, Scenario evaluation pipeline (D10).

- Constant-pace simulation on the projection loop (#219). `evaluateConstantPace` (`src/domain/simulation.ts`) evaluates the reference simulation: the real transactions up to today, the planned occurrences and the estimated variable spending of #218, through one call to `projectWithRouting`, and returns the daily balances, the routing of the planned transfers and every flow applied, in application order, with its source (`FlowSource` gains `estimate` and `modification`) and its category; the two legs of each placement of a planned transfer are not external and carry no category. `projectWithRouting` takes an optional last argument: `simulatedFlows`, applied after the planned occurrences of their day in the order given, and `steps` (`BalanceAwareSteps`), called once per day of the range with the running balances, whose transfers (`transfersOn`) are routed along the overflow chain by the same router as planned transfers and reported in `simulatedTransfers`, and whose credits (`creditsOn`) apply last, unrouted. A simulated transfer whose amount is not a positive integer, or a step movement dated another day than the one asked, is refused with a `RangeError`. Without that argument the outputs are unchanged, so the real chart, the calendar and the line view show nothing estimated (ADR-011). See DOMAIN, Scenario evaluation pipeline (D5).

- Estimate of variable spending for simulations (#218). `estimateLines` (`src/domain/variable-estimate.ts`) computes one estimate line per account and category (uncategorized spending of an account included) from the expenses of active accounts not linked to a planned item: the average of the complete months of the window (3, 6 or 12, `ESTIMATION_WINDOWS`) that start on or after the opening date of the account, rounded with `divideRound` and never below zero, with the number of months used, replaced by an override when the user set one. `estimatedFlows` spreads each line into daily flows over a range: what remains of the current month from today to its end (the spending of the month already made, never below zero, is subtracted), then each later month in full, with `spreadEvenly` giving the remainder one minor unit at a time to the first days. A date that is not a calendar date, or a monthly amount that is not a whole number of minor units of zero or more, is refused with a `RangeError`. Nothing in the ledger, the pages or the charts uses it yet (ADR-011). See DOMAIN, Estimated variable spending.

- Replacing a planned transfer marked as transferred by its bank row (#210). The import review also flags a new savings row that may be a planned transfer already marked as transferred by hand, comparing the savings row as a whole (`savingsRowTransaction` in `src/domain/planned-matching.ts`) with the hand-entered outgoing legs of the occurrence on the same source account within the window and tolerance of the planned item, whatever either split along the overflow chain (owner decision Q1 of #210: offered whatever the split, and after Replace only the split of the import remains). It offers the same two choices ("Replace my entry with this bank row" or "Keep both") and the same pre-selection rule as #179, and the server checks each choice again on commit (a stale choice blocks the file with nothing written). On Replace, `replaceEnteredTransfer` (`src/ledger/planned-matching.ts`) deletes every hand-entered pair of the occurrence in one statement and links every outgoing leg of the imported row to the occurrence, with no category inherited, before the matching and the digest; the split of the savings rows, the ceiling warnings and the gap with the balance assertions are computed without the replaced legs on every account of the chain. Messages in English and French. See DOMAIN, Importing files.

- Deleting a planned item created by mistake (#180). `deletePlannedItem` (`src/ledger/planned-items.ts`) removes the overrides, the versions and the item in one database transaction, as long as no transaction references the item (`hasLinkedTransactions`, checked inside that transaction, so a link recorded after the page was shown still refuses it with `PlannedItemHasTransactionsError`); an unknown id throws `PlannedItemNotFoundError`. The edit page has a "Delete" section, active or cancelled item alike: a sentence says what goes, and a secondary "Delete" button opens a confirmation that says the deletion cannot be undone; on success a toast confirms it and the page goes back to `/planned`, as it does with a toast when the item is already gone. An item with a linked transaction shows a sentence instead of the button: it can only be cancelled, or stays cancelled. The "Cancel" section is unchanged. Messages in English and French. See DOMAIN, Planned item and versions.

- Replacing an entry marked as paid by its bank row (#179). The import review flags a new expense or income row that may be a payment already marked as paid by hand on the same account (`findEnteredPayments` in `src/domain/planned-matching.ts`, within the window and tolerance of its planned item around the entry) and offers "Replace my entry with this bank row", chosen in advance when there is one candidate, or "Keep both". The choices travel in the commit request (`replacements`, checked again by the server: a stale choice blocks the file); `commitImport` deletes the entry and links the bank row to its occurrence, giving it the category and description of the entry when it has none, before the matching and the digest. Undoing the file does not bring the entry back, as the review and the undo panel say.

- Marking a due occurrence as paid, and linking a transaction from the occurrence (#178). `markOccurrencePaid` (`src/ledger/planned-matching.ts`) records the real transaction of a planned, partial or overdue occurrence and links it in one database transaction: the account, kind and name of the item, the default payment mode, the category of the item inherited as by a match, the planned date and amount or the ones typed. An income above the deposit ceiling is recorded with a warning in the toast. A provision takes one more payment and stays partial until nothing remains; a settled, cancelled or unknown occurrence and an archived account are refused, with nothing written. A planned transfer goes through the manual transfer flow (`recordManualTransfer` and the "Above the ceiling" step) and its outgoing legs settle the occurrence. The "To pay" page on `/planned/due` lists the due occurrences (effective date today or earlier, oldest first) and those of the next 7 days (`dueOccurrences`, `DUE_SOON_DAYS`), each card with its date and amount editable and a button named by its type ("Mark as paid", "Mark as received", "Mark as transferred"); the focus moves on to the next card. The sidebar has a "To pay" entry with the count of due occurrences, the More list of `/settings` a row with the same count, and the calendar header a "To pay (n)" link. In the line view and the day details, each open occurrence gets the button of its type, which records the planned date and amount at once, and "Link a transaction", which opens `/planned/link?item=<id>&date=<nominal date>`: the unlinked transactions of the account of the item with the sign of the occurrence, filtered by label and by a date range that starts as the matching window (the period of a provision), and a Link button that goes back to the calendar. The actions of a table row now sit in a row of their own under it, and "Change" is a secondary button like the others. Messages in English and French. See DOMAIN, Matching planned vs actual.

- Matching of planned transfers (#175). At each import, `applyImportMatches` (`src/ledger/planned-matching.ts`) also matches the savings rows of the batch against planned transfers: a row split along the overflow chain is compared as a whole (the sum of its outgoing legs, whatever the split) with the same window, tolerance and decision as other items, and its outgoing account must be the source account of the planned transfer. A transfer leg only settles a planned transfer and a planned transfer only transfer legs; every outgoing leg of the row carries the link and the incoming legs none, so the row counts once, and no category is inherited. The import review lists a transfer row as one entry with the row sum, and a confirm or an undo acts on all its outgoing legs. A planned transfer is never a provision: the planned item validation refuses it ("A transfer cannot be a provision") and the form hides the provision box and label filter for a transfer. A transfer still cannot be linked by hand, and a transfer leg cannot be unlinked by hand either (`unlinkByHand` refuses it): the import review undoes the whole row. See DOMAIN, Matching planned vs actual.
- Default payment mode (#181). The payment mode preselected in the add-transaction form (card until changed) is a setting, chosen in a "Default payment mode" section of `/settings` with the payment mode chips and an apply button that posts without JavaScript; a toast confirms the save and an unknown mode is refused with nothing stored. The form still lets each transaction take another mode, and the next entry starts again from the default. Stored in `src/ledger/default-payment-mode.ts` (`getDefaultPaymentMode`, `setDefaultPaymentMode`).
- Changing a single planned occurrence from the calendar (#174). A "Change" button on each planned row not linked to a transaction and not on an archived account, in the line view and in the day details of the grid, opens a dialog that moves the occurrence to a new date, changes its amount, skips it, or restores the date and amount its schedule computes; the other occurrences stay as they are. `saveOccurrenceOverride` and `deleteOccurrenceOverride` (`src/ledger/planned-items.ts`) validate the nominal date against the schedule and refuse an occurrence linked to a transaction (it is unlinked first, owner decision Q1 of #174; a partial provision counts as linked, confirmed by the owner), or one of a cancelled item or an archived account; the new date may fall outside the span of its version or after the end date (Q2). See DOMAIN, Occurrence override. The amount is typed without a sign and signed by the kind of the item, read on the server with its currency. Calendar entries now carry `override` and `isEditable`, computed from the same checks as the server.
- Calendar month grid on `/calendar?month=YYYY-MM&account=checking|<id>` and the Calendar tab (#173). Each day shows its end-of-day balance, the counts of real and planned entries in words, "Below zero" and the unplaced surplus in text, and links to the line view without JavaScript; with it the grid is one tab stop with the date picker keys (arrows, Home, End, Page Up and Down clamped to the month), and Enter, Space or a click opens the entries of the day under the grid with the columns of the line view. A view switch (Grid and Lines) and a link to the planned items sit in the header of both views, and the grid has a compact phone cell. The wide sidebar order is Overview, Calendar, Transactions, Accounts, Imports, Planned, then To categorize and Settings, and the phone tab bar is Overview, Calendar, Accounts, Imports. `formatAmount` takes an optional `currencyDisplay` ("code" for the name of a cell). "To pay" comes with #178.
- Monthly line view on `/calendar/lines?month=YYYY-MM&account=checking|<id>` (#172). `getCalendarMonth` (`src/ledger/calendar.ts`) reads one month: every day with its real transactions and dated planned occurrences (status in words: planned, partial, confirmed, overdue, cancelled) and the end-of-day balance, shown on the last row of each day, with "Below zero" written in text, a line naming the first day below zero, and the unplaced surplus of a planned transfer written with its amount and the account that keeps it. The balance covers the active checking accounts in the reference currency, summed with the stored exchange rates (a currency without a rate is left out and named), and an account selector shows one account in its own currency; a missing or invalid month shows the current month. Nothing from past spending adds a row to a future month (ADR-011). The page header is an h1 "Calendar" with the month as h2, the `calendar` messages exist in English and French, and `resolveItem`, `linksByItem` and `toProjectionAccount` (`src/ledger/projection.ts`) are now exported. The month grid is `/calendar` (#173), and the "Mark as paid" and "Link a transaction" buttons come with #178 (owner, 19 Nov 2026).
- Planned months on the real chart (#171). The chart continues with the dated planned items from the current month, drawn dashed with a distinct marker, the last real point staying the last complete month. A horizon control (3, 6 or 12 months, 6 by default) is applied in the browser on one server read, the data table follows it, and the lowest planned checking month end is highlighted. The planned part is hidden when no planned item changes a month end. See DOMAIN, The real chart.
- Projection of the daily balances of the real trajectory (`src/domain/projection.ts`). `plannedFlows` turns the occurrences into dated flows (only `planned` and `partial` ones, at their remaining amount, a planned transfer giving two opposite flows), `projectDailyBalances` adds them to the real transactions to give the end-of-day balance of each account from its opening date, and `firstDayBelowZero` finds the first day the sum of some accounts is strictly negative. An occurrence whose date has passed but that is still unsettled, and the remaining amount of a partial provision whose period is still open, are counted at today. Nothing is estimated and no deposit ceiling is applied yet. See DOMAIN, Daily balance of the real trajectory (#169).
- Routing of planned transfers along the overflow chain in the projection. `projectWithRouting` (`src/domain/projection.ts`) applies real transactions and planned occurrences in one global order by effective date (on the same day: real first, then planned items by id, then by nominal date) and splits each planned transfer along the overflow chain of its destination from the running balances of its day. The source is debited only by what is placed, and the surplus that fits nowhere stays on the source and is reported with the placements on the record of the transfer (`PlannedTransferRouting`). `overflowChainProblem` (`src/domain/overflow.ts`) names what is wrong with a chain (cycle, currency, archived account): such a transfer is reported as invalid and nothing moves. `plannedFlows` and `projectDailyBalances` stay unrouted, and an income or interest above a ceiling is applied as is. See DOMAIN, Daily balance of the real trajectory (#170).
- Linking and unlinking a transaction to a planned occurrence by hand, from the edit page of the transaction (#168). `listLinkCandidates` (`src/ledger/planned-matching.ts`) lists every occurrence of a non-cancelled planned item on the account of the transaction, of the same sign, that is neither settled nor cancelled (a provision is unsettled while it is still planned, partial, or overdue with nothing linked: one with linked transactions whose period has ended is settled), nearest date first, with no window and no amount tolerance. A transfer and an already linked transaction have no candidate. A manual link never changes the category of the transaction nor the planned item, a regular occurrence refuses a second transaction (checked on the server at each write), and unlinking a manual link leaves the category alone. See DOMAIN, Matching planned vs actual.
- Review of the planned matches of an import on the card of a recorded file (#167). The panel "Matched with planned items" lists each automatic match with an Undo button that names the transaction, and "To confirm" shows one fieldset per ambiguous group with one radio per possible pair and "Leave unmatched"; a sentence says so when nothing is matched or proposed. Results are announced in a polite live region and the focus moves to the panel heading after a confirm or an undo. The server derives the proposals again on every read (nothing proposed is stored), checks on confirm that the pair is still proposed (a stale pair is refused with a message) and lets an undo act only on a transaction of that batch. An undone automatic match is proposed again, with no refusal remembered. The links stay out of the import digest, but a confirmation that gives the transaction the category of the planned item is a change of category, so it blocks the undo of the file (ADR-017), and the panel says so before the confirm when the transaction has no category. See DOMAIN, Importing files.
- Planned items matched at import (#166). `commitImport` now runs `applyImportMatches` (`src/ledger/planned-matching.ts`) in the transaction of the file, after the rows are written and before the digest: confirmed matches and provision links are written, proposed groups are only counted (`proposedCount`, for #167). The request carries `today`, the result `matchedCount`. A transaction without a category inherits the one of the planned item, flagged by `category_from_planned_item`, and the flag is reset whenever the category is changed by hand. `linkTransactionToOccurrence` and `unlinkTransaction` link and unlink one transaction: an unlink clears the category only when it came from the match. The import toast shows the matched count, in English and French. A link is not part of the digest of the undo, a category inherited at import is (ADR-017). See DOMAIN, Matching planned vs actual.
- Decision of planned versus actual matches. `decideMatches`, `isWithinTolerance` and `provisionLinks` (`src/domain/planned-matching.ts`) split non-transfer transactions into confirmed pairs, proposed groups to settle one at a time and unmatched ones: a pair is confirmed only when it is unambiguous, provision candidates join the same decision, and a transaction that fits a provision and a regular occurrence, or two provisions, is proposed. See DOMAIN, Matching planned vs actual.
- Provisions: a planned item settled by several transactions (fuel planned at 90 for the month, paid in several fill-ups). `provisionPeriod` and `isProvisionCandidate` (`src/domain/provision.ts`) give the period of an occurrence (the calendar month for a monthly repetition, otherwise the day after the previous nominal date up to its own, the first one having the length of the others) and select the transactions of the same account and category, dated in the period, whose label contains the optional filter (the "contains" operator of the categorization rules, now shared as `labelContains`). `resolveOccurrences` derives the remaining amount (the amount minus the linked sum, never past zero, sign kept; 0 once the period has passed), the `partial` status and `ResolvedOccurrence.period`, and the linked sum feeds the rolling average of a confirmed provision. The create form of `/planned` has a "Provision" checkbox and a label filter (the matching window and tolerance do not apply to a provision), the edit page shows a provision as such and lets the filter be edited, and the flag cannot be changed after creation. Messages in English and French. See DOMAIN, Provisions.
- Rolling-average amounts in `resolveOccurrences` (`src/domain/occurrence-resolution.ts`): the expected amount of an occurrence of a rolling-average version is the average, rounded at every step, of the previous `averageWindow` values of its version (paid sums, or the estimates of planned occurrences; cancelled and overdue ones are skipped), the starting estimate when there is none. A new version restarts the window, an override amount applies to its occurrence only and the window is filled from before the requested range. See DOMAIN, Amount of an occurrence.
- Versions, end date and cancellation of a planned item. The pure `validatePlannedItemVersion` and `validatePlannedItemEndDate` (`src/domain/planned-item.ts`) refuse a version before the start date or after the end date, on a cancelled item or on an item whose account is archived, a zero amount or one with the wrong sign, a repetition on a one-off item, and a date that would rewrite history: an occurrence on or after it that is settled or has an override, with the earliest allowed date in the message (the day after the latest one). A new version inherits the amount mode and the average window of the version in effect, and one on a date that already has a version replaces it. The ledger gets `addPlannedItemVersion` (which reports whether it replaced a version), `setPlannedItemEnd` and `cancelPlannedItem`, each in one transaction, and nothing is written when a validation refuses. The edit page of an item shows its versions (in effect, upcoming) and has the sections "Change from a date", "End date" (repeating items only) and "Cancel" (with a confirmation), in English and French. See DOMAIN, Planned item and versions.
- The `/planned` screen: a list of planned items (name, type, schedule in words, account, amount, a link to edit) and a form to create one (expense, income, interest or transfer; a one-off event or a monthly, last-day, every N months, quarterly, yearly or every N weeks repetition; a business-day rule; the matching window and tolerance in a closed "Matching" section, 5 days and 5 percent by default). The amount is typed without a sign and the type gives it, the tolerance is typed as a percentage and stored in basis points (`parseTolerancePercent` in `src/domain/planned-item.ts`, read by string handling, never through a float). The edit page changes the name, the category, the window and the tolerance, and, in sections of their own, the amount and the schedule from a date, the end date and the cancellation (see the versions entry below). The page is linked from the home page for now: it is meant to be reached from the calendar and from the wide sidebar (owner, 19 Nov 2026, added with #173), after the design pass of #176. The type segments are those of the add-transaction form and the list stays a `DataTable` on a phone, as the issue asks, rather than the two-line rows of the design reference.
- Planned items in the JSON export: the `plannedItems`, `plannedItemVersions` and `occurrenceOverrides` tables, and `plannedItemId`, `occurrenceDate` and `categoryFromPlannedItem` on each transaction. `schemaVersion` is now `2` and `docs/export.schema.json` is regenerated. Amounts are integer minor units with the currency of the account of the item, the tolerance is in basis points. The encrypted snapshot and its restoration are unchanged, and the JSON export still cannot be restored.
- Storage of planned items: the `planned_items`, `planned_item_versions` and `occurrence_overrides` tables and the `planned_item_id`, `occurrence_date` and `category_from_planned_item` columns of transactions, created by the migration `0013_planned_items` (existing transactions keep both links empty). `createPlannedItem` validates an item and writes it with its first version in one transaction, the pure `validatePlannedItemInput` refuses what DOMAIN forbids (the tolerance is in basis points, the average window is 3, 6 or 12, a provision needs a repetition), and `toPlannedSchedule` feeds `generateOccurrences`. No screen uses them yet.
- Archiving an account is refused while a planned item that is not over still has it as account or destination (an item is over when it is cancelled, or when it has an end date and its last non-cancelled occurrence is before today). `archiveAccount` takes today as an argument, read in the server action in the time zone of the server.
- Status of the occurrences of a planned item (`src/domain/occurrence-resolution.ts`): the pure `resolveOccurrences` links the transactions to the occurrences of `generateOccurrences` by nominal date, in one pass in nominal-date order, and derives the status (`planned`, `confirmed`, `overdue`, `cancelled`, and `partial` for provisions). A settled occurrence stays `confirmed` even when the item or the occurrence is cancelled afterwards, and the matching window is inclusive: `planned` on its last day, `overdue` the day after. A second link on a regular occurrence keeps it `confirmed` and lists both. `findOrphanLinks` reports the links whose nominal date the schedule no longer produces, which are ignored for the statuses. See DOMAIN, Occurrence status.
- Occurrence generation for planned items (`src/domain/occurrences.ts`): `generateOccurrences` turns a planned schedule (versions with their own repetition, business-day rule and amount), its recorded overrides, a date range and today into the sorted list of occurrences. A version applies from its effective date up to the day before the next one; a version that changes the repetition restarts its series, one that only changes the amount keeps its phase. The business-day rule gives the date and the nominal date stays the identity. A cancelled override or a cancelled schedule keeps the occurrence flagged `isCancelled` (nothing after today for a cancelled schedule), and the effective date decides membership of the range. Amounts are the version or override amount; the rolling average is not computed here. See DOMAIN, Generating occurrences.
- Recurrence rules for planned items (`src/domain/recurrence.ts`): `nominalDates` turns a repetition (one-off, monthly on day N or on the last day, every N months, quarterly, yearly, every N weeks) into its nominal dates inside a range, and `adjustToBusinessDay` moves a weekend date to the previous Friday or the next Monday (no public holidays). The start date is a lower bound. The calendar helpers live in `src/domain/calendar-date.ts`, shared with the date picker. See DOMAIN, Generating occurrences.

- Editable type vocabulary for imports: the words of a category that give the type of a row now have three levels, the fixed French and English words, the words learned from the categories of earlier imports (only words the fixed vocabulary does not know, so the fixed word wins on a disagreement) and the user's own words, which win over both. A section of the `/rules` screen lists every word with its origin written in text, and adds a word, changes its type, removes it or restores a removed one. The reason shown for a guessed row says where the word comes from. The user's words are kept in the settings, so they are part of the JSON export and the snapshot, and no migration is needed.
- Reading of any CSV file, in the pure domain: the decimal mark is read per file (`1.234` is a thousand in a file that writes `-12,50`), lines above the header, total rows and a summary block beside the table are ignored and reported with their lines, and the type, payment mode, category and description columns are found from their header words before their values (`CB`, `PRLV` and `VIR` are read as payment modes). A row with no label takes its category as label. The import dialog reads its files with it; its notices (columns ignored, values not recognized, assumed year, total rows ignored) are not shown yet.
- Overflow account chosen during an import: when a savings row exceeds the ceiling of an account that has no overflow account, the review dialog shows the amount that does not fit, in text, and asks which account takes it. The choice is only remembered in the dialog: the account chosen becomes the overflow account of the savings account for good when a file is imported, in the same transaction as the import, so closing the dialog or a refused import leaves no link behind. The dialog asks again only if that account is full in turn and has none. A savings-type account in the currency of the savings account can be created from the same place, and is created with the import too. A choice the ledger refuses (loop, archived or foreign-currency account) refuses the whole import with a message in the card, and each file of a multi-file import carries the choices, which an earlier file may already have saved. The usual rules apply (no cycle, same currency, no archived target), and the account that receives the file is not offered.
- Savings rows in an import: a row typed as savings is recorded as linked pairs of transfers, split along the overflow chain from the current balances, and a positive savings row is a withdrawal from the savings account. Rows are taken in date order, and each file is one batch, deduplicated against the account and recorded all or nothing: a part that fits nowhere, a loop in the chain or a row the ledger refuses blocks the file. The import dialog shows it (see the review dialog below).
- Import dialog with accounts and savings: any file chosen on `/imports` opens the review dialog (a CSV whose columns are not found still goes back to the column mapping form). Two selects sit at the top: the account that receives the files, which reads the files again when changed (duplicates and currency depend on it; the edits made on the rows are kept, see below), and the savings account, preselected with the head of the overflow chain when there is exactly one and limited to the active savings accounts of the same currency other than the receiving account. Each row has an editable type (expense, income, savings), category (not for savings), payment mode and, for savings, its own savings account. A savings row shows its split in text before the import ("€100.00 to Savings A, €200.00 to Savings B", or "€50.00 from Savings A" for a withdrawal), including the part that fits nowhere. The files are listed by their first date and each is planned from the balances the previous one leaves, so the "Import this file" button of a card stays disabled, with a sentence naming the file, until every earlier card is imported, cancelled or failed. A row whose type was guessed with little evidence reads "To confirm" with the reason in text, an unreadable row keeps its raw date and a type that contradicts the sign of the amount is marked "To confirm" with its reason (see Changed). When the ledger refuses a file, the dialog lists what blocks it and nothing is recorded.
- Column, selection and filter controls in the import dialog. Each CSV card has a "Columns of this file" block with one select per role (type, payment mode, category, description), set to the column the detection found: choosing another column, or "No column", reads the file again on the server with the same reader and recomputes its rows (the changes made to its rows follow their line in the file, see below). A column is offered to one role at a time. Every row has a checkbox with a label, a checkbox in the header selects all the rows shown, and the number of selected rows is announced as text. A group form changes the type, the category or the savings account of the selected rows at once, with the rules of a single row (the sign follows the type and a contradiction is marked "to confirm", only expenses and incomes take a category, only savings rows keep a savings account). A checkbox shows only the rows to confirm; the count of the file, the number of rows shown and the filter use the same definition. Focus is given back after a new reading, after a group change and when the last row to confirm leaves the filter.
- Corrections survive a column change or an account change in the import dialog. The type, category, savings account and texts a user changed on a row are tied to its line number in the file and applied again after the file is read with another column, or after every file is read again for another receiving account, even if the row moved in the list. A correction whose line the new reading no longer has is dropped, and the card says in text how many changes were kept and how many were dropped, until the next change made to the file. If reading again fails, the card shows the error as an alert, keeps its rows, and its "Import this file" button stays disabled until a reading succeeds. A "Read the file again" button next to the error reads the file with the assignment shown, so going back to the earlier columns does not require choosing another value; the error disappears once the reading succeeds, and the focus goes back to the button (error kept) or to the title of the card.
- Known limits of the import dialog: there is no way to add an overflow account or create an account from the dialog (a part that fits nowhere only blocks the file), the reading notices of the CSV reader are not shown, a CSV without a header and a currency column taken for a category are not handled, and a correct guess cannot be confirmed (a row stays "to confirm" until its type is chosen). Changing the account that receives the files reads every file again and drops the edits made in the dialog, but each file is read with its own column choices. The split displayed is computed in the browser by the same pure planner as the server, from the balances read when the files were read; the server plans again from the real balances at import and has the last word.
- Gap with the real balance at the end of an imported file: after a file is recorded, its card asks for the balance the bank shows at the end of the file and tells, in text, whether Demain holds more, less or the same. The end date is the last movement date of the file, and the typed amount is read without rounding.
- Balance assertion from the import review: a button records the real balance typed for the end of a file as a balance assertion dated on the last movement of the file, replacing an assertion of the same day.
- Repository structure: license (AGPL-3.0), `.github/` templates and CI skeleton, contribution and security policies.
- Import: once a file is recorded, the (label, category) pairs of its categorized rows are proposed as `equals` rules for its account. Nothing is created without selection, and pairs already covered by a rule are not proposed.
- Undo of an imported file from its card on `/imports`: every movement of the batch (both legs of a transfer included) is removed in one operation, only while nothing in it changed since it was recorded. A changed batch, or one recorded before undo existed, is refused with the reason. The rules, balance assertions and overflow accounts created with the file stay.
- Domain of the Summary balances: the real account balances of the sheet's Summary tab are read (separator detected per file, months without a balance reported as such) and planned as balance assertions at the last day of each month, refusing an archived account, a month before the account opened and a month not over yet. A preview lists what would be created, replaced and refused, and the save records everything in one transaction, all or nothing.
- Balances of the Summary tab, on a `/balances/summary` screen (linked from `/balances`): choose the CSV file, the account and an optional year, check a preview that says in text, for each month, whether its balance will be created, will replace the one of that day, is refused (account archived, month ended before the account opened, month not over) or is nothing to record, then record everything in a second step, all or nothing. The screen also compares the Summary with the history stored in Demain month by month (net, savings, cumulative figures and real savings balance), with the signed gap written out. It replaces the Summary part of `/migration`, which stays until it is retired.
- Pure comparison of a file under review with the balance assertions of its period: the movements the file would record (savings splits and rows already held included, taken from the plan of the review), the period it covers, and for each active account the computed balance against the real one with the gap and its direction, zero tolerance, plus how many assertions match.
- Import review: each file card shows whether the file leaves the balance assertions of its period, with a summary ("2 of 3 assertions match") and a table of the date, the real balance, the computed balance and the gap, written in words with who holds more. Files are compared in order, and the figures follow every edit made in the review.
- Processing order of several files in the import dialog: when more than one file is chosen, the dialog lists the files in the order they are processed (by the date of their first movement, ties keeping the order chosen), says in text when that is not the order chosen, and warns when the dates of a file overlap those of a file listed before it. Each file is processed whole, so on overlapping dates its savings rows are placed after all those of the earlier files, and the deposit ceiling is read in that order.

The split is still computed by the server.
- Transactions leaves the phone tab bar for the More list of `/settings`, to make room for Calendar (#173). The tab bar is now Overview, Calendar, Accounts, Imports; the wide sidebar keeps Transactions, after Calendar.
- Next.js is updated from 16.4.3 to 16.4.6, which includes the November 2026 security fixes (cache disclosure in `use cache`, SVG rejection bypass in image optimization, response forwarding in self-hosted apps).
- The review of an imported file is a page of its own, `/imports/review`, reached from the import, instead of an 80rem dialog (DESIGN.md section 8, "Not a dialog": work that needs more than 420 px is a page). The cards, the rows, the overflow choices and the Import and Cancel actions of each file are unchanged, and nothing is recorded or requested while rows are edited. A Cancel button leaves the page and drops what was not imported (it reads "Back to the import" once every file is imported or cancelled). The page has one `h1`, which takes the focus when it opens, and the headings of the cards go down one level to follow it. The review is kept in the browser between the two pages, so reloading the review page loses it, as closing the dialog did. See ADR-016.
- The balance check of a recorded import takes a Credit or Debit side (native radios) instead of a typed minus sign. The server refuses a typed plus, minus or Unicode minus sign, wherever it sits (before or after a currency symbol), and applies the side to the integer minor units (Debit 12,50 is -1250). 
- The amount cell of an import review row is typed without a sign (a typed sign is refused with a polite message). The Type column gives the direction of an expense or an income, and a savings row has a Credit or Debit control (Debit is a deposit). The sign of the file still fills the cell and the type; the side is split from the text with string handling only.
- List screens take the data display of the identity (DESIGN section 8). Transactions, accounts, currencies, rules and their vocabulary, the monthly synthesis, balances, the Summary balance preview and the to-categorize queue share one table: a visible caption in medium weight, uppercase muted column headers, 48 px rows with a hairline above and a tone change on hover, figures right-aligned, all inside a `rounded-card` card (16 px of padding on a narrow frame, 24 px from 768 px, a container query) that scrolls sideways by itself, so no page scrolls sideways at 375 px. The scroll region is focusable and named by the caption. A status is a word in a pill (an archived account, the vocabulary origin, a transaction waiting for a category in the warning tone) and the sidebar count and the empty lists use the shared count badge and empty state; an empty list offers one secondary action that goes to its add form. The tables have new captions and the empty states new action labels, in both languages.
- The appearance and language controls moved from the header to the Settings page. The header link to Security is replaced by the Settings button (Security is in the "More" list). The login page no longer has a language selector: it follows the browser language.
- Dialog and toast take the identity. The confirmation dialog is a 420 px card (`rounded-card`, overlay shadow, backdrop token) that fades and rises in, then fades out before the native dialog closes and gives focus back to its trigger; Escape plays the same exit. A toast is a card with a tinted disc holding a check or a warning icon, the kind word, a two-line text slot and a 44 px close button. A toast that the server renders enters with the stylesheet, one that the browser adds enters and leaves with Motion. Behavior is unchanged: success is a `status` that leaves after 8 seconds unless pointed at or focused, error is an `alert` that stays until closed. The ease of `src/app/motion.ts` is typed as a cubic bezier tuple, the shape Motion accepts.
- The interface takes the visual identity: the design tokens (`src/app/tokens.css`, now the source of truth) and the Tailwind entry built on them, light and dark following the system setting, Host Grotesk self-hosted as a WOFF2 subset that keeps the French thousands separator, the primary action in ink instead of blue, and the focus ring on the focus token. Tailwind's default palette, sizes, weights, radii and shadows no longer compile, every class of `src/` is migrated, and a test refuses the old classes, `duration-*` and hex colours in components. The real chart draws its first series in ink and its second muted until the trajectory chart replaces it.
- Balance assertions of an archived account can no longer be deleted (adding or replacing was already refused): they stay visible, shown as archived with the account, and the form and delete buttons are hidden on its balances screen. Nothing is stored for this, the state comes from the account.
- In the import dialog, an explicit type (the type column of the file or the one chosen in the row) wins over the sign of the amount: the row is recorded with the sign of the type (an expense is negative, an income is positive, the absolute value is kept), marked "To confirm" in text with its reason, and its file is no longer blocked. The amount cell shows the amount that will be recorded, and the duplicate fingerprints follow it. Savings are not affected.
- The monthly synthesis converts accounts and flows in another currency with the stored exchange rates instead of leaving them out, and its reference currency is the setting of the `/currencies` screen instead of a fixed EUR.
- Next.js is upgraded from 16.4.0 to 16.4.3, which fixes three advisories reported by `npm audit` (two unauthenticated remote code execution flaws in the image optimization API and in `use cache`, neither used by Demain, and a denial of service through Server Actions, which Demain exposes). The minimum version in `package.json` is raised to match.
- Archiving an account is refused unless its computed balance is exactly zero (a negative balance included). No transaction can be entered on, edited on or deleted from an archived account, and the currency of an account cannot change once it has a transaction.
- Field errors of the forms are announced to assistive technologies and focus moves to the first invalid field after a refused submit.
- Validation errors of the domain carry a translation key (`MessageRef`) besides their English message, and form errors are translated server side.
- "Today" and the current month of the transactions form and of the monthly synthesis follow the time zone of the server (`TZ`) instead of UTC.
- The sheet migration reads the type of a row without a type column with the layered guess: a file with no negative amount now gives expenses (it gave incomes), while a signed file still gives expenses for negative amounts and incomes for positive ones.
- The sheet migration reads files through the shared CSV reader: a tab with lines above its header, a total row or a summary beside the table is read without the unreadable rows they used to cause (each is reported), a row with no description takes its category as label and is reported to be read instead of being skipped, a header that names the type, the payment mode or the category now selects that column, the number format of the file settles amounts such as `1.234`, and an amount that breaks the format of the file is reported as unreadable instead of being guessed on its own.
- An incoming savings row is no longer guessed as an income to confirm: savings are accepted in both directions. The sheet migration keeps its single run and its all or nothing (ADR-016).

- The messages that describe the problems of a Summary file now belong to the Summary balances screen (`summaryBalances`), so that retiring the migration leaves `/balances/summary` fully translated.
- The first-run rule of the sheet migration (refuse an account that already has transactions) is replaced by the deduplication of the import. Opening balances are set only when an account is created.

### Fixed

- A lone CSV file whose columns had to be mapped by hand is now read again with that mapping and goes through the import review, instead of being recorded straight from the mapping form (#209). It skipped everything the review offers, including the flag of a bank row that matches an entry marked as paid, so such a payment was counted twice. The mapping travels with the staging request (`stageFileAction` reads the mapping fields), the review keeps it for each file and reads the file again with it, and it is recorded with the file at import as before. A mapping that gives one column to two roles, or that reads no row of the file, is refused on the form with the columns kept as typed (new messages in English and French). The mapping form now records nothing (`readMappingAction` only gives the first rows and the fields to fill), and its Import button, sample rows and the messages that only served them are removed. See DOMAIN, Importing files.
- An end date earlier than the nominal date of the last skipped, modified or linked occurrence of a planned item is now refused, with the earliest end date allowed in the message (#204). The schedule stopped producing that occurrence, so its override or link disappeared from the calendar and came back if the end date was later extended. An end date on that nominal date, an occurrence moved past the end date and clearing the end date stay allowed.
- The link of a transaction to a planned item (`transactions.planned_item_id`) now restricts deletion in the migration `0013_planned_items`, as the Drizzle schema already declared (it was created with the default `NO ACTION`). The migration is edited in place because it has not been released: a database created before this change keeps `NO ACTION`, which refuses the same deletions.
- The login and setup pages are rendered per request instead of at build time. They read the database, which does not exist while the Docker image is built, and Next.js 16.4.6 failed the build on the missing data directory.
- Client navigation no longer waits for the server: a click in the sidebar or the tab bar shows the destination at once, with its title, links, headings, intros and column headings, and the skeletons of its data, which the data then replaces. The session check moved from the top of each page into its data sections, inside their `Suspense` boundaries and still before any read of the database, and the `id` of the edit and balance pages is read there too, so each page is part of the App Shell that Next.js prefetches for every visible link. The security page now loads its two-factor section behind a skeleton, and the settings and backup pages check the session without holding the page. The root layout keeps `instant = false` only for direct visits, which render per request for the nonce of the content security policy; client navigations into every page are validated by `next dev`. See ADR-023.

- An overflow account must now be an active savings account: saving an account or restoring a backup refuses a checking account (or any account that is not of kind savings) as target, and the overflow account selectors of the account screens and the choice list of the import review only offer active savings accounts.
- The deposit ceiling check of an import now counts the incoming transfers of the account that receives the file, as DOMAIN says: a withdrawal from savings that brings the balance above the ceiling is reported as a warning, and a deposit to savings earlier in the file frees room.
