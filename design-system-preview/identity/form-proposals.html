Adding a transaction, three proposals

Today the form asks for eight fields in a column, five of them with long labels. The three proposals below ask for the same data but type only two things for a usual expense: the amount and, if needed, a label. Every choice is a tile or a chip with an icon, a colour and a word, so it can be recognised without reading and still read by a screen reader. All three are live: try them.

Retained flow: amount, what for, check and save

Chosen by the owner on 11 Nov 2026 from the proposals below.

  • Step 1, from C: the type selector, the large amount and the keypad. The amount can also be typed straight into the display with a keyboard; it keeps the same look and the same rule, digits and one comma or point.
  • Step 2, from B, unchanged: what it was, with suggestions from history, and the category grid.
  • Step 3, editable in place: the recap rows open their editor right there with "Edit", without going back. "Pick a date" opens the calendar of the date picker component. A transfer asks for the account that sends and the account that receives; the same account cannot be both. "More details" holds the note and the payment mode.

What the three share

  • Defaults that are right most of the time: type Expense, date Today, the account used last. Nothing to touch for the usual case.
  • The amount first, large, with its sign drawn from the type: a red minus for an expense, a green plus for income or interest. The field only takes digits and one comma or point.
  • One meaning per colour, as in the identity: red and a down-right arrow for money out, green and an up-right arrow for money in, ink for the chosen chip, amber for "Later" (the to-categorize queue), blue only on the Today chip.
  • The label suggests from history: picking "Carrefour City" fills the category through the existing categorization rule, and says so.
  • Category is a grid of icons, with "Later" as an explicit choice: skipping it sends the transaction to the to-categorize queue, as the app already does, but the user sees it.
  • Rare fields wait under "More details": note and payment mode. A transfer swaps the account for "From" and "To".
  • The button says what will happen: "Add expense of €42.00", not "Submit".

Proposal A

One screen, amount first

The whole form on one screen, in the order people think: how much, which way, what for. Defaults cover date and account; they stay visible as chips so a wrong default is caught at a glance.

Proposal B

Three short steps

    Proposal C

    Quick sheet with a keypad

    A sheet that slides up over any screen on a phone, like the bottom sheets of 07:32 and 12:59. A large keypad types the amount: it can only produce digits and one comma, so there is nothing to correct. The categories used most come first in a row.
